# Statistical Analysis of Climate Change Impact on Energy Consumption in Germany

## Overview
This project analyzes the relationship between climate change indicators, economic activity, and energy consumption in Germany using publicly available datasets. The study applies statistical analysis and regression modeling to understand how temperature, CO₂ emissions, and GDP per capita are associated with energy consumption patterns.

## Objectives
- To examine long-term trends in energy consumption in Germany
- To analyze the relationship between climate variables and energy demand
- To evaluate the role of economic activity in energy consumption
- To apply statistical and econometric techniques relevant to data science research

## Data Sources
- **World Bank Open Data**
  - Electricity consumption (kWh per capita)
  - GDP per capita (constant prices)
- **Our World in Data**
  - Average annual surface temperature
  - CO₂ emissions per capita

## Methodology
- Data cleaning and preprocessing
- Exploratory data analysis (EDA)
- Correlation analysis
- Simple linear regression
- Multiple linear regression
- Multicollinearity diagnostics using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)

## Key Findings
- Average annual temperature shows a statistically significant association with energy consumption
- GDP per capita is a strong explanatory factor for energy demand
- CO₂ emissions lose significance when economic factors are controlled for
- No serious multicollinearity was detected among explanatory variables
